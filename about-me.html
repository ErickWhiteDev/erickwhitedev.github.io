About Me

Hello! I am Erick White, an undergraduate aerospace engineering and applied mathematics major at the University of Colorado Boulder. My main interests lie in numerical and computational methods for astrodynamics, and more broadly in mathematical and physical modeling. I am dedicated to using my engineering education to influence change and drive forward human scientific innovation.

Throughout my undergraduate career, I have gained extensive experience in software engineering and research methods. As an intern for the NASA CARA (Conjunction Assessment and Risk Analysis) program, I worked with both existing code (unit testing, implementing edge case checks, code robustness, etc.) and new research code I developed myself (all of which was done in MATLAB). I returned to CARA for a second year as an analysis intern, a research-heavy position in which I authored a paper which was later accepted to the 2025 AAS/AIAA Astrodynamics Specialist Conference. I also worked as an undergraduate researcher at the Lightning, Atmosphere, Ionosphere, and Radiation Belt Research Group under the guidance of Dr. Robert Marshall as part of the competitive Discovery Learning Apprenticeship program; my work focused on visualizing and identifying electromagnetic ion cyclotron (EMIC) waves in the Earth's magnetosphere and training a computer vision model to detect these waves automatically in space probe data. Most recently, I have worked as an undergraduate research assistant in the Celestial and Spaceflight Mechanics Laboratory under the guidance of Dr. Daniel Scheeres; my work focuses on modeling and understanding the dynamics of asteroid energy and angular momentum transfers. I am currently in the senior year of my undergraduate studies. I have taken the bulk of the aerospace engineering and applied math major classes, as well as the classes required for the computer science minor, which I have completed; together, the skills I have gained from these have given me some of the necessary insight to work in both academic and industry environments.

One of my key strengths is my ability to work in a team, both as a leader and as a follower. When I am put in charge of a team, I am very effective at ensuring that work gets done and that all team members are heard; my feedback from my peers regarding my leadership abilities has been overwhelmingly positive, and I am often the one to assume leadership roles when no one else stands up to do so. Additionally, my experience as a recitation leader for the University of Colorado Boulder Engineering Honors Program has been valuable in teaching me how to teach and communicate effectively in small groups, a critical skill in any workplace. As a follower, I am always sure to communicate my progress, questions, and doubts to ensure an effective and efficient work environment; I am not hesitant to ask questions if I believe they need answering. Additionally, I always offer myself to help others in team environments, as I believe a team can only work effectively if all of its members understand what is happening and how to move forward.

Following my undergraduate education, I am looking to pursue a Ph.D. in astrodynamics; I am very interested in conducting research, as I believe it is critical to advancing science and technology for the betterment of humanity. I am hoping to work in a math-intensive research position with a focus on astrodynamics, although I am open to exploring what the world of aerospace engineering and applied math has to offer, especially as it pertains to modeling and simulation.
